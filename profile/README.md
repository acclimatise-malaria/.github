# ACCLIMATISE
 
**Attribution of a Changing CLIMate in the AssessmenT of malaria Intervention Strategy Efficiency**
 
An international research consortium coordinated by [ICTP](https://www.ictp.it/esp/acclimatise), September 2025 to August 2028, funded by the Wellcome Trust.
 
---
 
## About
 
ACCLIMATISE is a three-year project that combines modelling and observations to separate the climate signal from the effects of malaria interventions and of other environmental and socioeconomic changes. This organization hosts the code, models and tools developed within the consortium.
 
## Background
 
Large investments in malaria interventions have reduced the malaria burden. At the same time, the climate has kept warming, with associated changes in rainfall and weather extremes that add to year-to-year and decadal variability. These changes affect malaria transmission through the climate sensitivity of the parasite and its mosquito vector.
 
Climate change can therefore either enhance or offset the impact of interventions, and interventions can in turn be optimised to mitigate climate effects, for instance to prevent outbreaks at higher altitudes. Ignoring climate can lead to the impact of interventions being over- or under-estimated. Equally, neglecting interventions makes the attribution of malaria outbreaks to anthropogenic climate change inaccurate.
 
## Aims
 
- Use **counterfactual simulations** to attribute malaria trends in endemic settings to anthropogenic climate change, as well as extremes in highland areas and the monsoon fringe, under a range of intervention scenarios.
- Co-design an **AI emulator** with stakeholders to assess efficient intervention strategies in the present and the near future, to 2050.
- Provide the evidence for a fair **cost-benefit analysis** of the efficiency of interventions.
The project contributes to UN Sustainable Development Goals 3 (Good Health and Well-being) and 17 (Partnerships for the Goals).
